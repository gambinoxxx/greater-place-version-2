Why We Chose Ogene As Our Signature Piece

Rooted in Igbo musical tradition, Ogene brings percussive rhythm and call-and-response into the Greater Place repertoire — here's the story behind our most requested performance, and why it had to be this one.

Founder NameFounder & Artistic Director
Sep 10, 2026
6 min read

Every company has one piece that becomes its signature — the work people ask for by name, the one that ends up on the poster before it even opens. For Greater Place, that piece is Ogene. It wasn't planned that way. It happened because a rehearsal ran long one Thursday, someone started clapping a rhythm from home, and by the end of the night eleven dancers were building a piece out of nothing.

Ogene takes its name from the hand-held metal gong central to Igbo traditional music — an instrument used historically to call communities together, announce news, and set the pace for communal dance. The rhythm is built on call-and-response: a lead pattern is stated, and the group answers it, layering in polyrhythm until the whole stage is moving as one pulse.

Why it fits who we are

Greater Place was never meant to be a company that performs one style. Our dancers come from gospel choirs, hip-hop cyphers, ballet intensives and church dance ministries — and Ogene is the rare piece flexible enough to hold all of that at once. The call-and-response structure means every dancer gets a moment to lead and a moment to answer, which mirrors exactly how we run rehearsal: nobody just follows.

"The first time we ran it full-out, nobody wanted to stop. That's when I knew this was the one."

— Founder Name, Founder & Artistic Director

We spent four months building the piece with a percussion consultant before it was ever shown to an audience, adapting the traditional rhythm structure for a company of our size without losing what makes it feel alive. The costuming — layered wax-print fabric in deep red and gold — was designed to move with the polyrhythm rather than against it, so the eye reads the whole stage as one instrument.

What it's taught us

Ogene has become more than a performance piece — it's become a teaching tool. Younger dancers in the Discover and Develop stages of the Pathway learn the foundational rhythm before they ever learn choreography, because understanding the pulse changes how they move through everything else we teach. It's also, unsurprisingly, the piece our audiences ask about most after every showcase.

We'll be performing an extended version of Ogene at this year's Winter Showcase, alongside two new pieces built by our senior company members. If you've seen it before, this version goes further. If you haven't — we'd love for you to be in the room.
